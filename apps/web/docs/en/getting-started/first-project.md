# Write your first short story

Follow a lost-property story from premise and outline to prose and a local revision. The characters and requests are teaching examples; substitute your own story if you prefer.

## 1. Create the project

Choose **Short Story** on the workbench home, leave the idea empty, and select **Start Writing**. In the project switcher, edit the project name to **Before Closing**.

The starting folders are **Characters**, **Concept**, **Materials**, and **Drafts**. Keep approved characters and plans in the first two before drafting.

## 2. Discuss a three-scene outline

Send:

```text
I am writing a realist mystery, Before Closing, from attendant Lin He's limited viewpoint.
The station lost-property desk closes at six; it is now 5:40.
Shen Min claims a gray canvas bag. She describes its exterior correctly
but names the wrong item in its inner pocket. A verbal claim is not enough to release it.

Give a three-scene outline: goal, obstacle, and change in each scene.
Leave the reason for her mistake and the bag's final recipient undecided.
Discuss in chat only; do not draft prose or change project files.
```

Choose the cause and ending after reading. Tell the AI which suggestions you accept; keep unused alternatives out of established facts.

## 3. Save characters and the outline

Create files and paste approved text yourself, or ask the AI to create only these files:

| Folder and title | Type | Contents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Characters / Lin He | Character | Responsibilities, goal, and knowledge boundaries |
| Characters / Shen Min | Character | Chosen background and motive; open questions separately |
| Concept / Three-scene outline | Outline | Scene changes, stopping points, and final choice |
| Drafts / Scene 1 — The claim | Draft | The narrative text you adopt for this scene |

A title does not determine file type. Use Draft for prose, and the corresponding types for characters and outlines.

## 4. Draft the first scene

Save the character files and outline, open **Scene 1 — The claim**, and send:

```text
Read Lin He, Shen Min, and Three-scene outline first.
Draft only Scene 1 — The claim, around 400–600 words, in Lin He's limited viewpoint.
Advance through actions and questions. Stop when Shen Min names the wrong item.
Do not reveal the cause, explain her thoughts, or continue to Scene 2.
Write into the current draft file; do not create a duplicate.
```

Check the target file: did it reach the stopping point, add unapproved facts, or lose the transition between paragraphs? Length is a target; you decide which text to keep.

## 5. Revise one specific problem

Suppose the draft says “Lin He knew she was lying,” but you want ambiguity. Select the line and its necessary context, choose **Add to chat**, and send:

```text
Revise only the quoted passage. Lin He knows the description is wrong, not why.
Replace the accusation with an observable action or question. Keep the bag at the desk.
Suggest replacement text first; do not change other scenes or motives.
```

After adopting it, read the surrounding transition. In diff review, accept or reject each change before finishing; pending changes are adopted when you finish.

## 6. Save, continue, or export

Confirm the prose is saved before advancing to the next scene. Reuse approved notes and specify that scene's change and stopping point. When finished, choose **Export manuscript** in the project header or phone menu, then inspect scene order and the ending in the downloaded TXT.

See the [editor](../user-guide/editor.md), [AI assistant](../user-guide/ai-assistant.md), and [export guide](../user-guide/export.md) for detailed operations.
